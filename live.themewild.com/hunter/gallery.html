Why Panama Is Special

Small on the map. Huge for birds.

Panama sits between North and South America and between the Caribbean Sea and the Pacific Ocean. Lowland rainforest, mountains, mangroves, wetlands, coastlines and human landscapes occur within short distances.

It also lies along important migration routes. During seasonal movement, birds that breed far to the north may pass through or spend part of the year in Panama before continuing farther south.

Extreme habitat variety From humid lowlands to cool cloud forest, bird communities can change quickly with elevation.
A continental bridge The isthmus channels seasonal movement between North and South America.
Wetlands matter too Coasts, mudflats and wetlands can be essential feeding areas for migratory waterbirds and shorebirds.

Keel-billed Toucan

Keel-billed Toucans are colorful forest birds that eat fruit along with other foods. A toucan's large bill is lightweight for its size and can act as an efficient radiator for heat exchange.

Northern Jacana

Northern Jacanas are wetland birds famous for their extremely long toes. Those toes spread their weight across floating leaves, letting them forage on vegetation that would not support a compact foot.

Panama Fact 01

Panama records more than 1,000 bird species.

For a country of its size, Panama supports exceptional avian diversity because tropical lowlands, mountains, wetlands, coastlines and migratory routes occur close together.

Bird Anatomy & Adaptation

Every feature tells you something.

Bills, wings, feet, feathers and senses can reveal what a bird eats, how it moves, where it lives and how it interacts with its environment.

Explore a feature
Form & function A bill is a tool shaped by feeding, handling and survival.
Bills

The bill is more than a mouth.

Bill size and shape can hint at how a bird gets food. Hooked bills help tear, slender bills can probe or reach flowers, and broad or oversized bills can handle fruit and other resources.

Habitat Explorer

Four landscapes. Four different bird communities.

Habitat is one of the strongest clues in bird identification. Knowing where you are can immediately narrow what you are likely to see.

Habitat 01

Lowland Rainforest

Layered vegetation creates canopy, understory and forest-floor niches for very different birds.

Habitat 02

Cloud Forest

Cooler, wetter highlands support specialists such as the Resplendent Quetzal.

Habitat 03

Wetlands & Mangroves

Shallow water, mudflats and mangrove edges support herons, shorebirds, jacanas and many migrants.

Habitat 04

Gardens & Edges

Flowering plants, fruiting trees and edge habitat can attract hummingbirds, tanagers and adaptable species.

Migration Through Panama

The sky becomes a highway.

Panama's narrow position between continents concentrates migratory movement. Timing varies by species and weather, but seasonal patterns can dramatically change what observers see.

Conceptual route for learning. Exact pathways vary among species, populations, seasons and weather conditions.
September–November

Southbound migration intensifies.

Many migrants move toward tropical wintering areas. Panama can concentrate remarkable movements of raptors and also receives songbirds and shorebirds moving through the region.
