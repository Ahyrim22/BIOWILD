Amazing Bird Facts

Explore some of the remarkable birds, behaviors, and ecosystems that make Panama one of the most important places for bird diversity in the Americas.

Panama's National Bird

The Harpy Eagle

The harpy eagle (Harpia harpyja) is one of the most powerful eagles in the Neotropics. Panama has played an important role in conservation and reintroduction efforts for this extraordinary species.

Apex forest predator Specialized for hunting in tropical forests
Brilliant Adaptation

The Keel-billed Toucan

Its enormous colorful bill is more than decoration. Toucans use it to handle food and its large surface area also helps with heat regulation in tropical environments.

Built-in cooling system The bill helps transfer body heat
Tropical Intelligence

The Scarlet Macaw

Scarlet macaws are highly social parrots known for their intelligence, strong bills, loud calls, and long-term pair bonds. Their powerful beaks allow them to crack extremely hard seeds and nuts.

Complex social behavior Communication is essential to their groups
Flight Specialists

Hummingbirds

Hummingbirds are extraordinary flying specialists. Their wing movements allow them to hover in place and maneuver with incredible precision while feeding from flowers.

Precision flight Exceptional control in the air
Migratory Birds

Panama is a Bird Highway

Panama sits between North and South America, making it an essential stop for migratory birds. Coastal wetlands, mudflats, and mangroves provide critical feeding areas.

More than 1,000 species Recorded across Panama's diverse habitats
Meet the birds

Birds of Panama

From powerful raptors to colorful tropical species, Panama's ecosystems support an exceptional variety of birds.

Ramphastos sulfuratus

Keel-billed Toucan

A colorful tropical frugivore whose huge bill is used for feeding and thermoregulation.

Harpia harpyja

Harpy Eagle

One of the most powerful forest eagles of the Americas, specialized for hunting in the canopy.

Ara macao

Scarlet Macaw

A highly social parrot recognized for its brilliant red plumage, powerful beak, and loud calls.

Did you know?

Panama's birds are part of a global ecosystem.

Protecting birds means protecting the forests, wetlands, mangroves, rivers, and ecological corridors they depend on.

A bridge between continents

Panama connects North and South America, making it an important biological and migratory corridor.

Wetlands are critical

Coastal wetlands and mangroves provide feeding and resting areas for huge numbers of migratory shorebirds.

Forests sustain biodiversity

Tropical forests provide nesting sites, food, shelter, and ecological connections for countless bird species.

Birds on the move

Panama's Migration Story

Every year, millions of migratory birds travel through Panama during their journeys between breeding and wintering grounds.

A biological corridor

Panama's geographic position creates a natural bridge between two continents and several major bird migration routes.

Panama Bay

Panama Bay is internationally important for shorebirds. Large numbers stop there to feed during their migration.

Protecting habitat

Conserving forests, mangroves and wetlands gives migratory birds the resources they need to complete their journeys.

We are dedicated to protecting Panama's birds and their natural habitats. Our sanctuary provides a safe environment for injured, rescued, and endangered birds while promoting conservation, education, and respect for wildlife.
